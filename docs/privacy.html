Privacy policy

HomePlanner (ESP32 panel) · last updated 3 October 2026

HomePlanner is open-source software that runs on a calendar panel in your own home. This policy explains what it does with your Google account data. In short: your data stays between Google and your panel.

What HomePlanner accesses

When you choose Sign in with Google on the panel's settings page, you allow the panel to:

  • View and edit events on your calendars (calendar.events): to show the events of the calendar you choose, and to add, change or delete events when someone does that on the panel. If you choose other Google calendars to show, their events are read (never changed).
  • See the list of your calendars (calendar.calendarlist.readonly): so you can pick your family calendar and any others to show.
  • See your email address (email, openid): to show which account is connected.

Where your data goes

  • The panel talks directly to Google over an encrypted connection. There is no HomePlanner server; the developers never receive your calendar data, your email address or your sign-in.
  • The panel keeps a copy of upcoming events and its Google sign-in (a refresh token) in its own memory, so it works after a restart. Nothing is sent anywhere else.
  • During sign-in, Google returns you through a static page (wentzeld.github.io/homeplanner-esp32/oauth.html) that only passes a one-time code back to the panel in your home. That page stores nothing and the code is useless to anyone else.
  • Once a day the panel asks GitHub whether a newer HomePlanner version exists (no personal data is sent; GitHub sees your home's internet address, as with any website visit).
  • No analytics, no advertising, no tracking, no selling or sharing of data.

HomePlanner's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Removing access

  • On the panel's settings page, choose Disconnect Google: the panel deletes its sign-in and tells Google to withdraw it.
  • Or remove HomePlanner at myaccount.google.com/permissions.
  • Resetting the panel (erasing its flash memory) removes everything it stored.
